Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage
A highlight for wildlife lovers, Pinnawala is the world's
most renowned elephant orphanage and conservation
center. The main attraction is watching dozens of these
majestic animals being led to the river twice daily for
their public bath—a truly unforgettable spectacle as
they play, splash, and socialize in the water
Dambulla Cave & Golden Temple
A UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Dambulla Cave
Temple is a spectacular complex of five caves dating back
to the 1st century BC. Perched on a massive rock, it is the
largest and best-preserved cave temple complex in Sri
Lanka. The site is renowned for its breathtaking interior,
which houses over 150 stunning statues and murals of
Buddha and various deities, covering virtually every inch of
the cave ceilings and walls. The climb to the caves offers
panoramic views of the surrounding plains, including the
iconic Sigiriya
Rock in the distance.

Minneriya National Park
Famous for one of the world's most spectacular wildlife
phenomena, Minneriya National Park is a premier
safari destination. During the dry season hundreds of
Asian elephants congregate on the grasslands
surrounding the ancient Minneriya Tank to feed, bathe,
and socialize.
Even outside this peak period, the park offers excellent
elephant sightings, along with other wildlife such as
sambar deer, toque macaques, and a rich variety of
birds.

Sigiriya (Lion Rock)
A UNESCO World Heritage Site and Sri Lanka's most
iconic landmark, Sigiriya is an ancient rock fortress and
palace ruin rising dramatically 200 meters from the
central plains. Built in the 5th century by King
Kasyapa, this "Eighth Wonder of the World" is a
breathtaking feat of ancient engineering, urban
planning, and artistry
Village Experience
A village tour in Habarana offers an authentic and
immersive glimpse into the traditional rural life of Sri
Lanka's countryside. It's a hands-on cultural experience
that provides a peaceful contrast to the historical sites
and wildlife safaris. Typically, the tour involves a
bullock cart ride through lush paddy fields, a tranquil
catamaran (reed boat) ride on a local lake, and a visit to
a family home.

Nalanda Gedige
Nalanda Gedige is a unique and enigmatic ancient stone
temple, often described as Sri Lanka's best-preserved
medieval stone building.
Dating between the 8th and 11th centuries, it showcases
a remarkable fusion of Hindu and Buddhist
architecture, with elements reminiscent of South Indian
temples and classical Sinhalese design.
Spice & Herbal Garden
A visit to a Spice and Herbal Garden offers an
engaging, sensory introduction to Sri Lanka's famous
spices and Ayurvedic traditions. Stroll through lush,
cultivated gardens where guides explain the growth and
uses of plants like cinnamon, cardamom, pepper,
vanilla, and various medicinal herbs.

Temple of the Tooth
Located in the heart of Kandy, this is the most sacred
Buddhist temple in Sri Lanka. It is built around a
revered relic—the tooth of the Buddha—and is a living
center of worship and a UNESCO world heritage site.
The temple complex is a place of immense spiritual
energy, featuring ornate architecture, intricate wood
carvings, and beautiful murals depicting the relic's
history. The highlight is witnessing the daily
ceremonial rituals where the casket containing the relic
is venerated with great devotion amidst chanting and
drumming.
Kandy Gems Museum & Workshop
Visit to a Gems Museum in Kandy offers a fascinating
insight into one of Sri Lanka's most famous industries.
These venues typically showcase the journey of a gem
from a rough stone mined from the earth to a
beautifully cut and polished jewel. The tour often
includes a demonstration of the cutting and polishing
process, an explanation of how to identify different
gemstones, and a display of both raw and finished
stones, highlighting Sri Lanka's famous sapphires,
rubies, and cat's eyes.

Royal Botanic Gardens, Peradeniya
Located near Kandy, the Royal Botanic Gardens are the
finest and most extensive of their kind in Sri Lanka.
Spanning 147 acres and beautifully set in a loop of the
Mahaweli River, the gardens are a serene paradise of
meticulously maintained lawns, pavilions, and a vast
collection of tropical and subtropical flora. Highlights
include the majestic Avenue of Royal Palms, the
sprawling Great Circle lawn, a fascinating Orchid
House with numerous varieties, and a colossal Javan
fig tree with its extensive, captivating canopy. It's a
perfect place for a peaceful stroll, a picnic, or to escape
the city's hustle.
Ramboda Water Falls
Sri Lanka's most accessible and impressive waterfalls,
cascading approximately 109 meters down a rugged
cliffside.
Ceylon Tea Experience
A visit to Sri Lanka is incomplete without exploring the
world of Ceylon Tea. The hill country, around towns
like Nuwara Eliya and Ella, is blanketed with lush,
terraced tea plantations. A tour of a tea factory is a
fascinating journey from leaf to cup, where you can
witness the traditional withering, rolling, fermenting,
and drying processes that create the tea's distinct
character.

Rawana Waterfall
One of Sri Lanka's most iconic and widest waterfalls,
Ravana Falls is a spectacular sight, especially during
the rainy season. Plunging approximately 25 meters
in a dramatic cascade, it is named after the legendary
demon king Ravana from the Ramayana epic.
According to the myth, this is the area where Ravana
hid the kidnapped Princess Sita in the caves behind
the waterfall.
Diyaluma Falls
As the second-tallest waterfall in Sri Lanka, plunging
approximately 220 meters, Diyaluma Falls is a
breathtaking natural spectacle. Located near the town
of Koslanda in the Badulla District, it has become a
premier destination for adventure seekers and nature
lovers.
Unlike many waterfalls that are only viewed from a
distance, the unique appeal of Diyaluma is the ability
to hike to the top, where a series of stunning natural
infinity pools offer a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity
to swim while overlooking the dramatic escarpment
and the plains below.

Buduruwagala
Meaning "the rock of Buddhist sculptures,"
Buduruwagala is an ancient and serene rock temple
dating back to the 9th or 10th century. It features
seven magnificent figures carved into a single large
rock face, making it home to the tallest ancient
Buddha statues in Sri Lanka.
The central standing Buddha figure, at about 16
meters tall, is flanked by two groups of bodhisattvas.
Yala National Park
Yala is Sri Lanka's most famous and visited national
park, renowned for having one of the highest densities
of leopards in the world. Located in the island's
southeast, it encompasses a diverse landscape of
scrub jungle, saline lagoons, rocky outcrops, and a
stunning coastline. A safari through Yala offers an
exceptional wildlife experience, with the chance to
spot leopards, elephants, sloth bears, crocodiles, and a
vast array of birds.

Galle Fort
UNESCO World Heritage Site, Galle Fort is a
historic walled city founded by the Portuguese and
extensively fortified by the Dutch in the 17th century.
This living monument is a captivating blend of
European architecture and South Asian traditions,
perched on a peninsula overlooking the Indian Ocean.
Turtle Hatcheries
Turtle hatcheries along Sri Lanka's south-west coast
are conservation projects dedicated to protecting
endangered sea turtles. They work by collecting eggs
from vulnerable beaches, incubating them in a safe
environment, and releasing the hatchlings into the
ocean to increase their survival rates.
Madu Ganga River Safari
The Madu Ganga, located near Bentota, is a pristine
mangrove estuary and a Ramsar Wetland site, known
for its rich biodiversity and tranquil beauty. A boat
safari through its labyrinthine waterways offers a
peaceful escape into nature.
